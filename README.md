# Naungan

Privacy-preserving, anticipatory humanitarian cash. Agencies deduplicate their beneficiary lists without sharing them (private set intersection), enroll households as anonymous Semaphore commitments, a flood oracle opens claims when the **GloFAS** river forecast (the same model IFRC Early Action Protocols trigger on) crosses a return-period threshold, and households claim once from their phone with a zero-knowledge proof. Donors verify every total on-chain, with zero personal data there. Full pitch: [docs/PROPOSAL.md](docs/PROPOSAL.md).

**Try it live (Base Sepolia, Circle testnet USDC):** [landing](https://naungan.vercel.app/) · [public dashboard](https://naungan.vercel.app/dashboard?program=2) · gas relayer [naungan.vercel.app/health](https://naungan.vercel.app/health). One enrolled household card is left unclaimed for reviewers: [open the demo card](https://naungan.vercel.app/claim#p=2&id=sG0DdcsCNBfd7i%2FIGGuxLmkPFbIN2ydmlblYsEm3rK8%3D) on a phone, tap Claim, and 1 USDC lands in a wallet created on the phone, about 12 s end to end. First come, first served: a second claim is rejected by design.

```bash
npx hardhat node                                                  # terminal 1
npx hardhat run scripts/deploy.js --network localhost             # writes web/deployment.json
npm run dev                                                       # terminal 2 → http://localhost:5173 (Vite, hot reload)
node scripts/relayer.js                                           # terminal 3 → http://localhost:8788

node scripts/coordinator.js create --name "Krueng Aceh flood · Banda Aceh" --lat 5.55 --lon 95.32 \
     --return-period 5 --lead-days 10 --trigger 60 --amount 50 --claim-days 14 --season-days 180
node scripts/coordinator.js add-agency --program 1 --agency <agency address>
node scripts/coordinator.js mint --amount 1000 && node scripts/coordinator.js fund --program 1 --amount 250

# agency B (its own PRIVATE_KEY) answers dedup; agency A dedups against it and prints cards
node scripts/agency.js serve  --program 1 --list data/unhcr.csv --port 7001
node scripts/agency.js enroll --program 1 --list data/zakat.csv --peer http://localhost:7001 \
     --claim-url http://localhost:5173/claim --cards cards/zakat.html

node scripts/oracle.js --program 1                       # today's real GloFAS forecast
node scripts/oracle.js --program 1 --replay 2025-11-20   # replay the real 23 Nov 2025 Krueng Aceh flood
node scripts/oracle.js --program 4 --source rain --replay 2025-11-20 --dry-run   # the rain pipeline on the same event
node scripts/oracle.js --program 6 --replay 2024-04-15 --dry-run                 # Dhaka heatwave: replays the April 2024 heat BDRCS acted on
```

Open a card's QR (or paste its code at `/claim`) and claim. The services read `PRIVATE_KEY` from `.env` (gitignored, never committed); on a local node that wallet is given dev ETH automatically.

## What is real, and its limits

- **Trigger data** is live GloFAS v4 from Copernicus via Open-Meteo. A `--replay` report is labelled `REPLAY` in its on-chain evidence and never presented as a forecast.
- **Oracle trust** is an M-of-N quorum of keys chosen by the coordinator; each report is re-derivable from the public API and its on-chain evidence string. Program 4 runs live as **2-of-2 with two independent forecasts**: one key posts GloFAS (Copernicus/ECMWF river model), another posts NOAA GEFS rainfall averaged over five cells of the Krueng Aceh catchment. Both post from the hosted workflow every 6 hours, and the dashboard shows each oracle's reading, age and evidence.
- **Why the rain oracle uses a 1-in-2-year threshold and a catchment average:** replaying the real Nov 2025 flood, rainfall at the river-mouth cell was 240 mm against a 318 mm 1-in-5-year 10-day total, so a point gauge at 1-in-5 would have blocked the trigger while the river flooded. The catchment mean was 262 mm against a 229 mm 1-in-2-year total: the rain signal confirms GloFAS instead of vetoing it. ERA5 underestimates convective tropical rain, so a stricter rain threshold would miss real events.
- **Track record** replays the trigger over reanalysis, i.e. what a *perfect* forecast would have fired on. It shows how often a program would pay and that the quorum filters rain-only alarms; it does not measure forecast skill, and it is not checked against recorded impacts (GDACS lists only province-level Aceh floods and misses Nov 2025 near Banda Aceh, so it is too coarse to score hits).
- **Who can recognise a claim.** Nobody can from the chain. But a printed card's secret was generated by the agency that printed it, so that agency could compute the card's nullifier and spot the household's claim and payout address. Households with a phone can avoid this: an agency shows them a `claim#join=<program>` link, the phone makes the identity, and the agency enrolls only the commitment the phone shows (`commitment` column in `scripts/agency.js`). The agency then holds nothing that matches the claim. The trade-off is custody: the phone holds the only copy, so the app offers a backup card to print, and a household that loses both cannot claim. Cards stay available for households without phones.
- **Payout audit** re-verifies each proof against the final group: enrollment closes when the trigger fires and claims open only after it, so every valid claim proves membership of that one tree. It checks claims sent straight to `claim()` (what the relayer does); one routed through another contract shows as not directly decodable rather than as verified. By design it cannot tell which household claimed.
- **Global: 8 live programs in 4 countries on 3 continents.** Aceh is the pilot, not a limit, and every program uses the trigger of the local Red Cross or Red Crescent Early Action Protocol. **#7 Mpologoma river, Butaleja, Uganda** (Uganda Red Cross EAP: GloFAS ≥ 60% for a 1-in-5-year flood, 5-day lead; URCS activated on 15 Nov 2023 and GloFAS reanalysis crossed the level on 27–30 Nov) and **#8 Aguán river, Tocoa, Honduras** (Honduran Red Cross EAP: GloFAS > 50% for a 1-in-10-year flood within 3 days; replaying 14 Nov 2024, Tropical Storm Sara, crosses it at 3,621 vs 2,446 m³/s, and the record also catches Hurricane Mitch in 1998). The landing page lists every program from the contract with its country, rule and state; the country comes from its on-chain coordinates through bundled borders ([country-coder](https://github.com/rapideditor/country-coder), the ones OpenStreetMap's iD editor uses), so no geocoding service sees program locations or can rate-limit the page. Heat programs can also require warm nights (`--tmin`, Karachi's rule is above 42 °C by day and 30 °C by night); we did not launch Karachi because ERA5's 25 km grid runs too cool on that coast to ever reach 42 °C, even in the deadly June 2015 heatwave, and lowering the threshold quietly would misstate the rule. Two programs run in Bangladesh on the triggers the Bangladesh Red Crescent's own Early Action Protocols use: **#5 Jamuna at Bahadurabad** (GloFAS ≥ 50% for a 1-in-5-year flood within 15 days; replaying July 2020 crosses it, July 2024 peaks 1% short, when BDRCS acted on the national water-level forecast instead) and **#6 Dhaka heatwave** (above 38 °C on 2+ consecutive days; ERA5 shows 8 such heatwaves since 1984, every one of them since 2021, among them the April 2024 heat BDRCS activated on). GloFAS and NOAA GEFS are global, the contract takes any coordinates, and the landing page checks any river worldwide (Piura, Peru, comes out paying in the 1998 and 2017 El Niño floods). What is regional is name matching, so it has profiles; the `basic` profile has no tuning for local naming conventions beyond accents, case and honorifics, and a new region would get its own profile from its registers.
- **Dedup** is semi-honest DDH-PSI over exact and phonetic keys, tuned for Aceh registers: Fatima/Fatimah, Muhammad/Mohammed, titles (Teuku/T., Teungku/Tgk, Cut, Haji/H.) and bin/binti, and pre-1972 spelling (Oemar/Umar, Djamal/Jamal, Jusuf/Yusuf) all match. A birth year one off, or a one-letter typo Soundex misses (Dewi/Devi, Mulyadi/Muryadi, via SymSpell-style deletion keys), is a possible match: it is held back in `<list>.review.csv` for a person to confirm, never auto-merged, because namesakes in one village are also a year apart. Two typos in one word still miss; the typo keys make a dedup run ~15x larger (about 30 s for 200 against 200 households on a laptop). Claim cards and PIN slips print in Indonesian with English below.
- **PIN cards** (v2, `e2=`) need the relayer to test a PIN: the phone blinds (program, card, PIN) on ristretto255, the relayer's `/pin` multiplies by a key derived from its own private key and that card's id (so a made-up id buys no fresh tries) and counts answers per card, and after 20 (issuance included) the card is locked, so a thief with the card has a 20-in-a-million chance instead of the minute-long GPU search that breaks a PBKDF2-only card. The relayer never sees the PIN. Limits: the per-card count lives in Upstash Redis (free tier, keys prefixed `naungan:`) when `UPSTASH_REDIS_REST_URL`/`TOKEN` are set, as on the hosted relayer (`/health` reports `pinStore`); without them it is per process and resets on a restart. Checking a PIN needs a connection (claiming already does). Rotating the relayer key invalidates v2 cards, and a locked card means a new card from the agency. v1 cards (`e=`, PBKDF2 only) still open.
- **Event history** for the dashboard and relayer comes from the Blockscout indexer (`deployment.json.indexer`), falling back to 200-block RPC windows when it is unreachable.
- **The relayer** must be reachable from the household's phone; `deployment.json.relayer` points the claim app at it.
