The problem

Aid coordination today forces a trade-off between efficiency and safety.

When many agencies serve one crisis, families get paid twice or missed. The usual fix, one shared registry, concentrates the most dangerous data in the world in one place.

How it works

Four layers. No agency ever sees another’s list.

Each layer removes one reason agencies refuse to coordinate, and the chain only ever stores commitments, nullifiers and totals.

Private dedup

Agencies compare lists with private set intersection. Each learns only “already served?” for its own records. Fatima and Fatimah still match.

DDH-PSI · ristretto255

Anonymous enrollment

Each family holds its own credential, optionally PIN-locked. Agencies enroll only a cryptographic commitment into the program group.

Semaphore v4

Anticipatory trigger

The oracle reads 51 GloFAS river forecasts every 6 hours and posts the share that exceed the 1-in-5-year flood, with its evidence. Crossing the trigger opens claims days before the peak.

GloFAS · Copernicus

Private claim

A family proves “I’m enrolled and haven’t claimed” in zero knowledge, even offline. A relayer pays gas, yet can’t redirect the money, and the family spends it with no gas token.

Groth16 · EIP-3009
Private by construction

Donors see that it worked. Nobody sees who was helped.

Anyone can recompute “N unique households paid, X disbursed” from public events. The link between a family and its money never exists on-chain.

What the chain proves

  • Unique households paid ✓ public
  • Total funded and disbursed ✓ public
  • When and why the trigger fired ✓ public
  • No one claimed twice ✓ public

What it never learns

  • Names, ID numbers, locations ✗ never
  • Which agency serves which family ✗ never
  • Which family received which payout ✗ never
  • Any agency’s beneficiary list ✗ never
Pilot context

Starting in Aceh, Indonesia.

Rohingya refugees and flood-prone villages are served at once by UN agencies, NGOs and zakat institutions. Indonesia is not party to the 1951 Refugee Convention, so data exposure is especially dangerous here. The first pilot: one district, one NGO and one zakat partner, about 200 households.

Built, not just pitched

A working prototype you can verify yourself.

Real Groth16 proofs generated on the phone and verified on-chain, a hosted gas relayer, an authenticated agency dedup service, two independent flood oracles (GloFAS river discharge and NOAA GEFS rainfall, 2-of-2 quorum) running every 6 hours and 33 automated tests. Anyone can re-run an oracle's computation from the dashboard, in their own browser.
