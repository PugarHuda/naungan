# Naungan: three-minute pitch script

For the presentation-video round (1–3 Nov) and the final at SMU (26 Nov). Read it aloud over the deck (https://claude.ai/artifact/YC8mEsVfear6FnqXc8ZQPc); about 150 words a minute. Every figure here is in the deck's sources or the live app.

| Time | Slide | Say |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | cover | Naungan means shelter in Indonesian. It is cash that reaches families before a flood, heatwave or cyclone, coordinated across aid agencies without any of them sharing who they serve. |
| 0:15 | dup | Humanitarian cash fails three ways. Agencies that cannot see each other pay some families twice and miss others: in Ukraine, coordination stopped 35 million dollars of overlap in four months. |
| 0:30 | privacy | The usual fix pools everyone's lists in one database, and those databases leak: half a million of the most vulnerable people in the 2022 Red Cross breach. |
| 0:42 | late | And money arrives after the water. When WFP paid families along the Jamuna days before the 2020 flood peaked, they were 36 percent less likely to go a day without eating. |
| 0:57 | statement | Naungan fixes all three: coordinate without sharing, prove without exposing, pay before the peak. |
| 1:05 | how | Agencies compare lists with private set intersection: no name leaves an agency. Only an anonymous commitment goes on-chain. Two independent forecasts must agree before claims open. And the family claims from a phone with a zero-knowledge proof: paid once, and nobody can tell who was paid. |
| 1:30 | demo | This is live on Base today, with Circle USDC. A judge can claim the demo card on their own phone: one US dollar lands in about twelve seconds. |
| 1:40 | families | The family reads why the money came, in its own language, written by Claude from on-chain facts and shown only if every number checks out. Donors see where each gift went, and own a fraction of the program's impact certificate, a hypercert that anyone can re-check and evaluate on-chain. Families rate the aid anonymously, but provably. |
| 1:58 | record | Replayed over thirty years, the Aceh trigger pays seven times, including November 2025, and holds back eight rain-only alarms. |
| 2:08 | anywhere | It is not only Aceh: ten live programs in six countries, each on a Red Cross or Red Crescent trigger, and anyone can check any river. |
| 2:18 | new | Blockchains already move aid. WFP's Building Blocks shares lists among members and pays after a crisis; parametric insurance pays after the season. Naungan is the one that dedups without sharing, pays before, and lets anyone verify every payout without learning who was paid. |
| 2:38 | pilot | It costs about half a cent per family on Base, it is rehearsed on mainnet with real USDC, and an agency can follow a written pilot plan. The contract has no owner, so it keeps paying even if we stop. |
| 2:52 | close | Our ask: one national society or charity to run the first pilot with us. Try it at naungan dot vercel dot app. Thank you. |

Delivery notes: speak to one family's story, not the stack; say "zero-knowledge proof" once and explain it as "proves you are enrolled without saying who you are"; if asked about compliance, KYC stays with the enrolling agency, off-chain (docs/PILOT.md).
